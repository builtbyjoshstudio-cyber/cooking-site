One-Skillet Spicy Sausage Hash

Sausage, potatoes, carrots, sweet peppers, a jalapeño, onion, and garlic — all in one skillet, and the only thing you have to get right is the order things go in the pan. The sausage browns first so everything after it cooks in that fat. A splash of chicken stock goes in, the lid goes on, and it cooks down until the broth turns into a glaze.

Dinner tonight, plus three meal-prep lunches for the week.

What's on the Board

Protein

  • Smoked sausage

Dense Veg

  • Carrots (cut to ~1-inch pieces so they finish together)
  • Petite potatoes (cut to ~1-inch pieces so they finish together)

Aromatics & Heat

  • Sweet peppers
  • Jalapeño
  • Onion
  • Garlic

Pan & Finish

  • Cooking oil
  • Salt
  • Black pepper
  • Red pepper flakes
  • Chicken stock
  • Butter

Pan Order & Method

  1. Prep the whole board first: Cut carrots and petite potatoes to roughly 1-inch pieces so dense vegetables finish at the same time. Prep sweet peppers, jalapeño, onion, and garlic.

  2. Brown the sausage for the fond: Even though smoked sausage is precooked, brown it hard first so it leaves browned fond and fat on the bottom of the skillet. Pull it when browned.

  3. Carrots and potatoes next: Oil first so the seasoning sticks, then season with salt, black pepper, and red pepper flakes. Cook carrots and potatoes first because they take the longest.

  4. Layer in peppers, onion, then garlic last: Add sweet peppers, jalapeño, and onion once the dense veg has a head start—seasoning at every step. Drop garlic in last so it never burns.

  5. Deglaze and steam under the lid: Add a splash of chicken stock, scrape up the fond, and cover until the broth reduces down to a glaze coating the vegetables in the skillet.

  6. Taste, return the sausage, and finish with butter: Taste the vegetables and adjust seasoning, return the browned sausage to the skillet, and finish with butter so everything comes together glossy.

What to Look & Taste For

  • Oil the veg before seasoning so salt, pepper, and pepper flakes actually cling instead of falling to the bottom of the pan.
  • When the chicken stock cooks down from loose liquid to a glossy glaze, the hash is done.
